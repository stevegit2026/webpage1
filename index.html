Five Storylines

Ranked by impact on Sunday

  1. Quarterback · This week

    Tanner McKee is 0-3 as the starter, and Joe Milton III is waiting

    Tanner McKee is 0-3 as a starter after Sunday night’s 27-10 loss at Washington, and Jalen Hurts, who has blood clots in his lungs, is not coming back to fix it. McKee went 23-of-34 for 212 yards and an interception and was sacked five times, per the Inquirer. A strip sack late in the third quarter came with the Eagles down 13-10, and the Eagles converted 5 of 15 third downs. In the fourth quarter, a deep fourth-and-25 throw to Darius Cooper was intercepted, setting up Washington’s last touchdown. McKee now has 638 passing yards, two touchdowns and two interceptions this season, and said, “There’s nothing that defenses have done to stop us.” The Inquirer raised the question of whether the Eagles stay with him or turn to Joe Milton III, whom they signed off Dallas’s practice squad two weeks ago. The Eagles have not announced a decision for the Giants game. Counts differ: NBC Sports Pro Football Talk had four sacks and nine hits, while the Inquirer had five sacks and eight hits.

  2. Eagles injuries

    Barkley is on a pitch count, and the receiver room is thin

    Saquon Barkley played through hamstring and back injuries Sunday night and was held to 13 carries for 30 yards and a three-yard touchdown, plus one 24-yard catch, per the Inquirer. That was 14 touches after nine against Dallas, and he said, “my body’s not really where I need it to be right now.” He has 274 rushing yards on 56 carries this season, per AP. The Eagles are short elsewhere. DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks, their top two receivers, missed a second straight game, per the Inquirer, and Britain Covey, Jihaad Campbell and Jonathan Jones were also out in the final Oct. 30 report. Nothing has been reported on whether any of them return Sunday. Safety Michael Carter II came back from two missed games with an abdominal injury but left in the third quarter with another, per the Inquirer and AP. The first Week 9 practice reports come Wednesday, so every status on this page is last week’s until then.

  3. Opponent · Giants

    Giants are rested and in first place, with a QB who is 5-0 as a starter

    The Giants (4-2-1) arrive rested and alone atop the NFC East after a Week 8 bye. They are the first team in franchise history to win with three different starting quarterbacks in the first seven games, per Giants.com. Jaxson Dart is out for the season after knee surgery. J.J. McCarthy, acquired from Minnesota in late September, won his first Giants start 27-21 in overtime at Houston, going 20-of-28 for 252 yards per the Canadian Press, though another outlet printed 272. John Harbaugh is 15-3 after byes, but the Giants have lost 13 straight in Philadelphia. Malik Nabers has topped 100 receiving yards in four straight games, and Cam Skattebo ran for a career-high 124 against Houston. The Giants are banged up: Andrew Thomas (groin), Jermaine Eluemunor (calf) and Theo Johnson (neck) were listed in the bye-week coverage, running back Tyrone Tracy is out four games with a hamstring injury, and pass rusher Brian Burns is on injured reserve. The Giants then host Washington on Thursday night.

  4. The line · Odds

    Giants are 2.5-point road favorites, up from 1.5 on Oct. 31

    Oddsmakers make the Giants a narrow road favorite, and the number has crept up. A median of seven books on Oct. 31 had New York at minus-1.5, per scoresandstats. DraftKings showed minus-2.5 at 7:04 p.m. Sunday, per Sports Betting Dime, during the Eagles’ game. VegasInsider’s consensus at 12:23 a.m. Monday, after the game, also says minus-2.5, with the Eagles at plus-114 and the Giants at minus-135. The total is 40.5 in all three. Sources do not fully agree: Bet365, quoted by StatsInsider without a timestamp, lists the Giants at minus-1.5 with a total of 39.5. A mid-October FanDuel page listed the Giants at minus-2 with a total of 47, which is stale because it predates the Hurts injury. The Eagles have lost six straight and the Giants lead the division, yet the market still treats this as close to a coin flip. Lines will move after Wednesday through Friday injury reports, and Sunday’s inactives come about 90 minutes before kickoff.

  5. Last game · Trade deadline

    Six straight losses, and the Nov. 10 deadline is two days after kickoff

    The Eagles (2-6) have lost six in a row for the first time since 2012 and are 2-6 for the first time since 1999, both under Andy Reid, per NBC Sports. They sit three games behind the first-place Giants, and CBS Sports’ model gives them an 8 percent chance at the playoffs. The trade deadline is Tuesday, Nov. 10 at 4 p.m. ET. ESPN and NFL Network reported Sunday morning that the Eagles will not trade Saquon Barkley, and he said afterward, “I plan on being here.” The team has still sold: it traded center Cam Jurgens to Baltimore for draft picks, per AP. Pro Football Rumors lists Riq Woolen, Dallas Goedert and Landon Dickerson as possible moves, and says general manager Howie Roseman could also buy a player who fits his long-term plans. The only game before the deadline is Sunday’s, against Barkley’s old team, so how the Eagles look against the Giants could shape what Roseman does.
