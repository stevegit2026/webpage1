Five Storylines

Ranked by impact on Sunday

  1. Quarterback · Milton prep

    Milton makes his first start against a top-three pass defense

    Joe Milton III makes his first NFL start Sunday, and the Giants know it. Nick Sirianni said Friday the move was what he felt the team needed “at this particular moment,” while calling Tanner McKee “solid” in an 0-3 stretch, per NBC Philadelphia. He praised Milton’s “very talented arm” and said he can “process and make decisions.” Milton, 26, signed off the Cowboys’ practice squad on Oct. 16 and has 424 passing yards, two touchdowns, two interceptions and 66 rushing yards in five relief appearances, per Sports Illustrated. The Giants rank third against the pass, allowing 184.9 yards per game, per PhiladelphiaEagles.com’s Saturday preview, and coordinator Dennard Wilson says Milton “can throw the ball 100 yards” and brings “a different dynamic,” but the Giants will not change their approach. Eagles Wire argues Milton’s legs could open lanes for Saquon Barkley against a Giants run defense allowing 4.8 yards per carry. The Eagles are 32nd in yards and 30th in scoring, per NBC Philadelphia. McKee is questionable with a rib injury, and Jalen Hurts is out for the season.

  2. Eagles injuries

    Smith and Wicks are out; Barkley is on track to play

    The Eagles ruled out six players Friday: receivers DeVonta Smith (hamstring), Dontayvion Wicks (ankle) and Britain Covey (knee), linebacker Jihaad Campbell (hamstring/quad), defensive back Michael Carter (abdomen) and cornerback Jonathan Jones (hamstring), per PhiladelphiaEagles.com. Smith has now missed five games in six weeks and Wicks three straight, so Makai Lemon and Darius Cooper are set to start again, with Hollywood Brown and Elijah Moore also in the mix, per the team’s Saturday preview. Right tackle Lane Johnson is listed out by SportsLine, and Eagles Wire refers to his loss on the line; the only reason I found is SportsLine’s “personal” tag. Saquon Barkley (hamstring/back) was limited Friday with no designation, and the team says he is on track to play. The Inquirer’s Jeff Neiburg still expects a snap restriction. Tanner McKee (rib) is the only Eagle listed questionable. Kicker Jake Elliott and edge rusher Jonathan Greenard returned to full practice, while Jalyx Hunt stayed limited without a designation. NBC Sports’ betting page still lists Barkley, Smith, Wicks and Carter as questionable, which does not match the team’s final report.

  3. Opponent · Giants

    Skattebo is doubtful, and Nabers is a full-go

    Running back Cam Skattebo (hamstring) is doubtful, and the picture is murkier than the official report. Giants.com lists him as limited Friday, but asked whether he could do anything at practice, coach John Harbaugh said “No,” per Giants Wire, while adding “let’s keep it open.” Skattebo hurt the hamstring in overtime at Houston on Oct. 25 and leads New York with 418 rushing yards. Devin Singletary is expected to carry the load, and Tyrone Tracy Jr. is on injured reserve. Wide receiver Malik Nabers (knee) sat out Thursday, practiced fully Friday, and Harbaugh says he will be “full go.” Nabers was NFC Offensive Player of the Month for October, with four straight 100-yard games, per the Eagles’ preview. Quarterback J.J. McCarthy, acquired from Minnesota, won at Houston before the bye, and the Giants have scored 27 or more points in four straight games. Charlie Jones is questionable with a hand injury per the report, though Harbaugh called it a thumb, and safety Jevon Holland is the backup punt returner. New York has not won at Lincoln Financial Field since 2013.

  4. The line · Odds

    The Giants are 3-point favorites at most books, and sources split on the half-point and the total

    The Giants are road favorites of 3 points at most books Saturday, after a week spent between 3 and 3.5. Win Daily Sports’ table shows Giants minus-3 at DraftKings, FanDuel and BetMGM, with moneylines of minus-162 to minus-175 on New York and plus-135 to plus-144 on the Eagles. Fox Sports’ Gambler (Friday) and SI (Nov. 3) show minus-3.5, so the half-point is in conflict. The total conflicts too: 38.5 per Win Daily, Kalshi and the Gambler, 39.5 per SI. Kalshi’s prediction market gives the Giants about a 61 to 62 percent chance. The Giants are 5-2 against the spread this season and the Eagles 1-7, per SI and the Gambler. NBC Sports puts the look-ahead line with Hurts at Eagles minus-5.5, a swing of roughly eight points. All three Inquirer writers picked the Giants on Friday (27-20, 31-14, 27-20), while the Gambler’s computer model says Giants 24, Eagles 20 but picks the Eagles to cover plus-3.5. One caution: Win Daily stamps its prices “8:04 PM ET,” later than this page’s timestamp, so treat the exact prices as approximate.

  5. Last game · Trade deadline

    Six straight losses, and the Nov. 10 deadline is two days after kickoff

    The Eagles (2-6) have lost six in a row, the last two by 17 points each, and are off to their worst eight-game start since 1999, per NJ.com. Sunday’s 27-10 loss at Washington turned a 10-0 lead into 27 unanswered points. The offense averages 17.1 points per game, the fewest for any Eagles team since 2012, per the Inquirer, which has it 29th in scoring and last in yards. NBC Sports Philadelphia has it 30th in scoring and 32nd in yards, so the sources differ on rank. The Inquirer also cites a 42.6 percent pressure rate allowed, fourth-worst in the league. The trade deadline is Tuesday, Nov. 10, at 4 p.m. ET. A Bleeding Green Nation roundup lists Hollywood Brown, Riq Woolen, Dallas Goedert, Michael Carter II and now Tanner McKee as players other teams see as available, while Moro Ojomo is a different case because Howie Roseman wants him long term. Sunday is the last game before the Week 10 bye, and the Eagles have won 13 straight against the Giants, per PhiladelphiaEagles.com.
