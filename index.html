Five Storylines

Ranked by impact on the London game

  1. Offensive line · This week

    Lane Johnson retires, Cam Jurgens is traded, and the line starts over

    Two days after the Rams loss, right tackle Lane Johnson, a surprise inactive on Sunday, announced his retirement, saying he needed to focus on his mental health. A day later the Eagles traded two-time Pro Bowl center Cam Jurgens to Baltimore. He was in concussion protocol and had struggled since moving to left guard in Week 2. Philadelphia sent Jurgens and a 2027 seventh-round pick and got back a 2027 fifth-rounder and a 2028 second-rounder. Howie Roseman said the team was not looking to move him until the deal became a win-win within 24 hours. Rookie Markel Bell, a third-round pick, takes over at right tackle. Drew Kendall stays at center despite a knee injury that kept him out of Wednesday’s practice, and Willie Lampkin is expected to make his first NFL start at left guard. The Inquirer notes Jalen Hurts was pressured on 57.6% of his dropbacks against the Rams, so Jacksonville will test this group early.

  2. Eagles injury report

    Barkley and Smith sit out; Mitchell, Baun and Goedert return

    The Eagles’ first practice report of the week listed 11 players. Saquon Barkley and DeVonta Smith, both with hamstring injuries, did not practice, and PhillyVoice calls them the most likely to miss Sunday. NFL Network reported Barkley is week-to-week and that the injury is not considered long-term. Tank Bigsby went on injured reserve after core muscle surgery and is expected to miss four to six weeks, so Will Shipley and newly added Dameon Pierce are the healthy backs. There was good news: cornerback Quinyon Mitchell, linebacker Zack Baun, tight end Dallas Goedert and tackle Fred Johnson were all limited, and Goedert and Baun were back at practice after missing the Rams game. Hollywood Brown, Marcus Epps, Jonathan Greenard, center Drew Kendall and linebacker Jihaad Campbell did not practice.

  3. Offense · Identity

    The offense is 0-for-17 on third down and searching for an identity

    The Rams loss exposed more than one fourth-down call. Philadelphia has not converted a third down in its last 17 attempts, a streak that began in the first half in Chicago, and it failed to score in either second half. The Inquirer reports the Rams blitzed Jalen Hurts on 11 of 12 third-down dropbacks, and that the Eagles have not scored more than 24 points in a game. Columnist Jeff McLane argues the team must commit to one identity, either fully backing coordinator Sean Mannion’s under-center system or changing course, and says Mannion moved away from outside-zone runs once Barkley left. Sirianni said he would carry much of the blame and expressed confidence in Mannion. Hurts called the performance poor execution and pointed to a bad snap exchange before the failed fourth-and-goal.

  4. Opponent · Jaguars

    Jacksonville is 3-1 with the NFL’s second-stingiest defense

    The Jaguars arrive 3-1 and have won two straight over AFC contenders. Through four games they have allowed 53 points, second-fewest in the NFL, with six interceptions tied for the league lead and the second-ranked red-zone defense, according to the Inquirer. Trevor Lawrence threw for 227 yards without an interception in the win at Cincinnati and leads an offense that has scored 30 or more in two of its three wins, per PhillyVoice. Four Jacksonville pass-catchers already have at least 100 receiving yards and a touchdown. The secondary is thinner: safety Caleb Ransaw went on injured reserve, safety Eric Murray (concussion) missed Wednesday’s practice, and several cornerbacks were limited, including Jourdan Lewis and Montaric Brown with hamstring injuries. Pass rusher Josh Hines-Allen (groin) was also limited.

  5. Matchup · London

    Fly Friday, kick off Sunday, and a line that keeps climbing

    The Eagles will practice at home all week, fly Friday, land Friday night and have Saturday to adjust before the 9:30 a.m. ET kickoff, which is 2:30 p.m. in London, on NFL Network. Nick Sirianni said the plan felt best for the team, jet lag included. The Rams used a similar late arrival in Melbourne in Week 1 and lost 27-7 to the 49ers. Jacksonville is the designated home team and plays Houston at Wembley next Sunday. The line has climbed all week: the Jaguars opened as 3-point favorites, reached 4.5 by Monday morning, were 5.5 to 6 at five books later Monday, and sit near 7 on Wednesday, with a total of 42.5. The Eagles are 0-4 against the spread, while Sports Illustrated notes the Jaguars are 9-2 as favorites under Liam Coen.
