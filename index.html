Five Storylines

Ranked by impact on Monday night

  1. Last game · Week 2

    Nine seconds, 65 yards, one undrafted hero

    The Eagles are 2-0 after a finish that had no business working. Jalen Hurts threw two interceptions in Nashville, and the second, an underthrown deep ball to DeVonta Smith with the game tied 17-17, set up a Titans field goal and a 20-17 lead with 1:56 left. With no timeouts, Hurts went 6-of-8 for 61 yards on a nine-play, 65-yard drive. Smith made a 19-yard catch on third-and-6 to keep it alive, and Hurts hit Darius Cooper, an undrafted receiver with no catches in Week 1, in the back of the end zone for his first career touchdown with nine seconds left. Hurts finished 26-of-37 for 264 yards and two touchdowns, and Smith caught 10 passes for 117 yards. Outlets differ on who made the first interception (the Inquirer says Amani Hooker; others credit Marcus Harris), but agree on the outcome. The turf reportedly reached 157 degrees, and coach Nick Sirianni needed IV fluids afterward.

  2. Opponent · Bears

    Bears QB Caleb Williams is hurt; Tyson Bagent is next

    Chicago’s Week 2 loss got worse late in the fourth quarter when quarterback Caleb Williams hurt his right hamstring on a scramble and was carted off. NBC Sports reports it is a strain that could be as slight as Grade 1, with testing set for Monday. Coach Ben Johnson was relieved it was not a knee but warned a hamstring can be damaging too. Backup Tyson Bagent, who started four games in 2023, went 5-of-9 for 54 yards in relief, led two late drives into the red zone without scoring, and is preparing to lead the offense, according to 247Sports. The market has noticed: FanDuel opened the Eagles as 3.5-point road favorites, with a 43.5 total, per Yahoo Sports. The Bears scored a franchise-record 59 points in Week 1, then managed three in a 9-3 loss to Minnesota.

  3. Eagles injury

    Saquon Barkley’s stinger: MRI first, answers later

    Saquon Barkley’s status is the biggest open question on the Eagles’ side. He suffered a stinger, a nerve injury in his left arm, on the game’s first play after being tackled by Jeffery Simmons, and headed to the medical tent and then the locker room. CBS Sports saw him return after halftime with a neck roll under his shoulder pads, though Yahoo reports he was pulled again, and he finished with four carries for nine yards. Barkley told reporters he expects to be fine but will know more later in the week, and an MRI is planned. Tank Bigsby filled in with 13 carries for 33 yards and a touchdown, and Will Shipley added seven for 24. Whether Barkley practices this week will shape how the Eagles approach Monday night.

  4. Eagles offensive line

    A patched-up line took a beating from Jeffery Simmons

    Philadelphia’s offensive line is the quiet storyline of the week. Starting left guard Landon Dickerson went on injured reserve with a knee injury and will miss at least three more games, so Cam Jurgens moved from center to left guard and 2025 fifth-round pick Drew Kendall made his first meaningful start at center. Against Titans tackle Jeffery Simmons, the new group struggled: Simmons had seven pressures and three hits on Hurts, who was sacked three times and hit six times. The line also drew three penalties, and Eagles ballcarriers combined for just 78 yards on 27 carries. Jurgens compared the switch to relearning how to use your hands and feet, while Jordan Mailata pointed to continuity and film as reasons for optimism. The same starting five is expected to stay together while Dickerson recovers.

  5. Eagles injury

    Dallas Goedert’s knee thins the tight end room

    Tight end Dallas Goedert left the Titans game in the first half after taking a hard hit to his right leg on a block. He was evaluated in the medical tent, was listed as questionable to return, and did not come back. The Eagles have not shared details on how serious the knee injury is, so this week’s practice reports matter. The timing stings: Goedert opened the season with four catches for 77 yards and two touchdowns against Washington, including the 43-yard game-winner. Against Tennessee, the Eagles turned to Johnny Mundt and E.J. Jenkins, and depth is a concern because second-round pick Eli Stowers is on injured reserve. Between Goedert and Barkley, two of Philadelphia’s most productive offensive weapons head into a road game with question marks.
