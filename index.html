Five Storylines

Ranked by impact on Monday night

  1. Quarterback · This week

    Hurts goes on injured reserve, and Tanner McKee keeps the job

    The Eagles placed Jalen Hurts on injured reserve Saturday with a superficial blood clot in his calf, a reversal after ESPN’s Adam Schefter and NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport first reported that IR was not the plan. The move costs him at least four games: Carolina, Dallas, Washington and the Giants. With the Week 10 bye, his earliest return is Nov. 22 against Pittsburgh, and the team has set no timetable while it settles on treatment. Bleacher Report, citing that reporting, notes that a moderate course of blood thinners typically takes at least six weeks. That leaves Tanner McKee, who went 26-of-45 for 215 yards and a touchdown against Carolina in his first start of the season and was sacked three times. Behind him are rookie Cole Payton and Joe Milton III, signed Friday off the Dallas practice squad. Milton, a former Patriots sixth-round pick, could face his old team. Nick Sirianni said he will build plays around McKee’s strengths and that “everything’s on the table.”

  2. Eagles injuries

    The Carolina game adds four more names to the hurt list

    Hurts was not the only loss. Edge rusher Nolan Smith left Sunday’s game with a concussion, defensive tackle Jordan Davis was ruled out late in the fourth quarter, and receiver Dontayvion Wicks went down in pain after a catch-and-run. Running back Carson Steele was ruled out with a shoulder injury in the third quarter. Sources differ on Davis: the Inquirer calls it a leg injury, while the AP report on CBS Philadelphia says ankle. The backfield was already thin. Saquon Barkley (hamstring) missed his second straight game, and Will Shipley and Tank Bigsby were also out, so Dameon Pierce was the only running back to carry the ball. On the line, Landon Dickerson remains on injured reserve, and center Drew Kendall dressed but did not play. The good news: DeVonta Smith returned from his hamstring injury and led the team with seven catches for 75 yards and a touchdown. The first Week 7 practice report has not been released.

  3. Opponent · Cowboys

    Dallas brings the league’s worst defense to South Philly

    The Cowboys (2-3) were still playing at Green Bay on Sunday night when this page was published. They trailed 17-0 at halftime after two Dak Prescott interceptions, and returner KaVontae Turpin was ruled out with a rib injury suffered on a kickoff return. Before that, Dallas lost 24-16 at home on Oct. 8 to a winless Tampa Bay team starting backup Jalon Daniels, while Bucky Irving ran for 165 yards. Writing on NFL.com, The Athletic’s Jon Machota found the defense under first-year coordinator Christian Parker allowing 6.4 yards per play and a 120.5 opposing passer rating, both the worst in the league, with no interceptions. Owner Jerry Jones said the team is “right where we started” on defense, and Fantasy Nerds counted 12 missed tackles against the Bucs. Prescott threw for 316 yards in that loss, and CeeDee Lamb, who bruised his quad in it, played Sunday night. Dallas ruled out cornerback Cobie Durant and tackle Drew Shelton for Green Bay.

  4. The line · Odds

    The books flip: Dallas opens as a 5.5-point road favorite

    Hurts’ absence has reshaped the betting picture. On Sunday evening, DraftKings posted Dallas as a 5.5-point favorite with a 44.5 total, according to SportsBettingDime, and the Eagles at +200 on the moneyline. Not every outlet agrees yet. VegasInsider’s consensus page still showed the Eagles as 1.5-point favorites with a 47.5 total, but its last line move was dated Oct. 13, before the clot news, and Covers had not yet posted a spread. The DraftKings number is the freshest, and it will move with Dallas’ Sunday night result and the first injury reports. The division market moved the same way. After the Hurts news, Dallas went from +180 to even money to win the NFC East, while Philadelphia fell from +170, when it was the favorite, to +385, behind the Giants at +270, according to Cowboys On SI. The Giants (3-2-1) lead the division despite losing to New Orleans on Sunday, and the 2-4 Eagles sit half a game behind Dallas.

  5. Last game · Week 6

    Four straight losses, and an offense at its lowest since 1999

    Carolina beat the Eagles 25-14 at Lincoln Financial Field, extending the skid to four games for the first time since 2020 and the first time under Nick Sirianni. Philadelphia punted on its first five possessions and finished with 222 yards. Trailing 19-14 with 9:32 left, the Eagles took over at their 48, but a bad snap cost 12 yards, a third-down pass was deflected, and Braden Mann’s punt was blocked. Bryce Young then hit Tommy Tremble for the clinching touchdown. Young threw for 281 yards and two scores, and Chuba Hubbard ran for 120 yards, most of them after halftime. The Inquirer notes that the Eagles’ 874 yards over the four losses are their fewest in a four-game span since 1999. NBC Sports Philadelphia’s Reuben Frank adds that the 58 points over that stretch are the fewest in 19 years. Sirianni was blunt: “You’ve got to score points, and we’re not doing that right now.”
