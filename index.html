Five Storylines

Ranked by impact on Sunday

  1. Quarterback · This week

    Sirianni says “Tanner is our guy,” but McKee is 0-3 as the starter

    Nick Sirianni said Monday, “Tanner’s our guy. We’re not making any changes there,” and that offensive coordinator Sean Mannion will keep calling plays, per ESPN. Joe Milton III remains the primary backup. McKee is 0-3 as a starter after the 27-10 loss at Washington, where he went 23-of-34 for 212 yards with an interception and was sacked five times, per the Inquirer and Eagles Wire. His third-quarter fumble, with the Eagles trailing 13-10, gave Washington a short field, and Jake Elliott then missed a 41-yard field goal. The Eagles scored nothing after a 10-0 lead. ESPN has McKee at 639 passing yards, two touchdowns and two interceptions across his three starts, while 97.3 ESPN has 638. The team averages 17.1 points per game, 30th in the NFL. Sporting News says Philadelphia has nothing to lose by playing Milton or Cole Payton, but Sirianni has ruled out a change for now. Jalen Hurts is out for the season.

  2. Eagles injuries

    Barkley is on a pitch count, and the receiver room is thin

    Saquon Barkley played through hamstring and back injuries Sunday night and was held to 13 carries for 30 yards and a three-yard touchdown, plus one 24-yard catch, per the Inquirer. That was 14 touches after nine against Dallas, and he said, “my body’s not really where I need it to be right now.” CBS Sports expects him to try to play through it again against the Giants. The receiver room is still thin. DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks, the top two receivers, missed a second straight game, and Britain Covey, Jihaad Campbell and Jonathan Jones were also out Sunday, per PhiladelphiaEagles.com. Rookie Makai Lemon led the team with six catches for 57 yards. Safety Michael Carter II left the game in the third quarter with another abdominal injury. Nothing has been reported on whether any of them return Sunday, because the first Week 9 practice reports come Wednesday. Outlets also differ on Barkley’s snaps against Dallas, nine or ten.

  3. Opponent · Giants

    Giants return from the bye in first place, with Skattebo day to day

    The Giants (4-2-1) are alone atop the NFC East after the Cowboys lost to Arizona in overtime and the Eagles lost Sunday, per Giants.com. They came back from the bye Monday with running back questions. Cam Skattebo, who ran for a career-high 124 yards at Houston and slid down with hamstring tightness late, practiced off to the side, and John Harbaugh called him “day to day” with more information late in the week, per the New York Post. Tyrone Tracy Jr. is on injured reserve for at least four games, so Devin Singletary and Gregory Desrosiers are the only healthy running backs on the 53-man roster. Pass rusher Abdul Carter missed practice with an illness, and left tackle Andrew Thomas said he is optimistic about playing through his groin injury. J.J. McCarthy, acquired from Minnesota in September, won his first Giants start 27-21 in overtime at Houston. The Giants have lost 13 straight in Philadelphia, but Harbaugh is 15-3 after byes. Washington visits MetLife next Thursday.

  4. The line · Odds

    The Giants are favored by anywhere from 1.5 to 3.5 points

    Oddsmakers make the Giants a narrow road favorite, but the sources disagree on how narrow. BetMGM had New York at minus-3 on Monday, with the Giants at minus-165 and the Eagles at plus-135, and a total of 40, per the Arizona Republic. Sports Illustrated’s Tuesday article lists minus-3.5 (minus-102), Giants minus-170, Eagles plus-142 and a total of 39.5, without naming the book. Kalshi and Polymarket both set the spread at 2.5, with totals of 39.5 and 40.5, per silive, which notes Polymarket’s spread and total markets had barely traded. PredictionMarketsPicks lists DraftKings, FanDuel, Caesars, BetRivers and Novig at minus-1.5, without dates. The Dimers model projects Giants 21, Eagles 19, which would make Philadelphia a 54.6 percent cover at plus-3, and ESPN gives the Giants a 54 percent win chance. Two odds pages carried bad data and were discarded, and SI also misstates the Eagles’ record as 1-3. Lines will move after injury reports.

  5. Last game · Trade deadline

    Six straight losses, and the Nov. 10 deadline is two days after kickoff

    The Eagles (2-6) have lost six in a row for the first time since 2012, per NBC Sports Philadelphia, and CBS Sports ranks their offense 32nd at 265.3 yards per game. Sirianni said “everything’s on the table” on scheme and practice but would not change coordinators, and Inquirer columnist Marcus Hayes called for Sean Mannion to be fired. The trade deadline is Tuesday, Nov. 10, two days after this game. NFL Network reported Sunday that the Eagles will not trade Saquon Barkley, per ESPN and 6abc. The team already traded center Cam Jurgens to Baltimore. NFL.com lists tight end Dallas Goedert, linebacker Zack Baun and defensive lineman Moro Ojomo as possible sells. ESPN says general manager Howie Roseman, who usually buys at the deadline, could also add a player who fits the long-term plan. Sunday is the Eagles’ last game before their Week 10 bye, so the result could shape what Roseman does. Giants.com says the Giants face Washington next.
