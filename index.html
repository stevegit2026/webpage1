Five Storylines

Ranked by impact on the London game

  1. Last game · Week 4

    Up 20-10, then 0-for-12: the Eagles hand the Rams a win

    The Eagles are 2-2 after a loss that looked safe at halftime. They led the Rams 20-10 and never scored again, finishing with 172 total yards and a 0-for-12 day on third down. Across their last two games they have converted just 2 of 21. The turning point came with 7:31 left, when Nick Sirianni passed on a chip-shot field goal that would have made it 23-10 and went for it on fourth-and-goal from the 2. Jalen Hurts’ pass to Darius Cooper fell incomplete, and Hurts pointed to a bad snap exchange on the play before. Sirianni took the blame. Los Angeles answered with a 98-yard touchdown drive, then a second long march, and Kyren Williams scored twice in the final four minutes. Inquirer columnist Marcus Hayes argues the call ignored how many starters were missing. PhillyVoice called the decision defensible, though it still graded the coaching a C.

  2. Eagles injury · offense

    Barkley’s hamstring, and a receiver room without Smith

    Saquon Barkley grabbed his right hamstring after a six-yard run in the first quarter, visited the medical tent and was ruled out with about four minutes left in the half. ESPN reported no timeline, so his status for London is open. Tank Bigsby and Will Shipley carried the load as the team ran for 104 yards on 27 carries, but Bigsby also needed help leaving the field late. The passing game was already short-handed. DeVonta Smith, who leads the NFL in target share at 32.9% through three games, sat out with a right hamstring injury, and league sources told the Inquirer he could miss two to three games. Dallas Goedert and Hollywood Brown were also out, and with Lane Johnson out for personal reasons, rookie Markel Bell started at right tackle. Hurts finished 12-of-27 for 93 yards.

  3. Eagles injury · defense

    Mitchell and Greenard leave, and the defense runs out of answers

    The defense played well for three quarters, with two first-half interceptions of Matthew Stafford that led to 10 points, and then lost two of its most important players. Cornerback Quinyon Mitchell hurt his knee tackling Blake Corum on the first play of the third quarter and was listed as questionable to return, and Jonathan Jones took his place. Edge rusher Jonathan Greenard, who made his season debut against the Bears after a pectoral injury, went to the locker room with an ankle injury and was downgraded to out. The Eagles had already played without linebacker Zack Baun (concussion, his first missed game this season) and safety Marcus Epps (groin). Stafford finished 32-of-51 for 317 yards, the Rams gained 415 yards and converted 7 of 16 third downs, and Jeremiah Trotter Jr. had 15 tackles. Reports gave no timeline for Mitchell or Greenard.

  4. Opponent · Jaguars

    Jacksonville is 3-1 and takes the ball away

    The Jaguars arrive in London at 3-1 after a 22-17 win at Cincinnati, their second straight victory over an AFC contender and their 11th in 12 regular-season games. Trevor Lawrence was 18-of-23 for 227 yards and a touchdown with no interceptions, though he was sacked four times. Chris Rodriguez Jr. broke a 25-yard touchdown run in the fourth quarter, and the defense forced two Joe Burrow interceptions and held on when a fourth-and-10 pass came up a yard short. Jacksonville has intercepted a pass in nine straight games, the longest active streak in the NFL, and Hurts threw interceptions in two of Philadelphia’s last three games. The Jaguars are not unhurt: safeties Caleb Ransaw (knee) and Eric Murray (concussion) left the Cincinnati game, and cornerback Montaric Brown missed it with a hamstring injury.

  5. Matchup · London

    A 9:30 a.m. kickoff in London, with the Eagles as underdogs

    The Eagles travel to Tottenham Hotspur Stadium for a 9:30 a.m. ET kickoff, which is 2:30 p.m. in London, on NFL Network. Jacksonville is the designated home team and plays Houston at Wembley the following Sunday, so the Jaguars are on London’s schedule for two straight weekends. Oddsmakers opened Jacksonville as a 3.5-point favorite with a total of 42.5, with the moneyline at Jaguars −180 and Eagles +150, according to SportsBettingDime, and a prediction market shown by Covers gives the Jaguars a 64% chance. Those numbers can move once Saquon Barkley’s and DeVonta Smith’s statuses become clear. Philadelphia has beaten Jacksonville in London before, 24-18 at Wembley in 2018, and this is the Eagles’ third regular-season game abroad, after that one and the 2024 game in Brazil. One odds page still shows Jaguars −3 with a 44.5 total but lists both teams at 2-1, so it predates this week.
