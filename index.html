Five Storylines

Ranked by impact on Sunday night

  1. Quarterback · This week

    Hurts is out for the season, and Tanner McKee makes start No. 3

    Jalen Hurts will miss the rest of the season. What the Eagles first called a superficial blood clot in his left calf turned out, after more testing on Oct. 19, to be a deep vein thrombosis and bilateral pulmonary emboli, according to a joint statement from the team and his agent. He is on blood thinners, is stable with no significant functional limitations, and the team expects a full recovery. NFL Network’s Ian Rapoport reported the treatment will take at least three months, per 6abc. League sources told CBS News Philadelphia he is out for the year, while AP says only that he is out “likely” for the remainder. That leaves Tanner McKee, who has completed 61.3 percent of his passes for 426 yards, two touchdowns and an interception in two starts, per AP. Against Dallas he went 23-of-35 for 211 yards with one touchdown and one interception and led the Eagles’ first third-quarter touchdown drive of the season. Sunday is start No. 3, against a Washington defense that blitzes 43.9 percent of the time, second-most in the NFL, per PhiladelphiaEagles.com.

  2. Eagles injuries

    Barkley is cleared, but both top receivers are out

    Saquon Barkley is expected to play. He sat out Wednesday and Thursday with hamstring and back injuries, practiced on a limited basis Friday, and ESPN reports he carries no injury designation. Earlier coverage was gloomier: delawareonline said Thursday his chances were “diminished,” and PhiladelphiaEagles.com called him uncertain on Thursday afternoon. Accounts of his Monday also differ. ESPN and AP have him at 62 yards on seven carries, while NFL.com has 71 yards on nine touches. Around him the Eagles are thin. DeVonta Smith (hamstring), Dontayvion Wicks (ankle), return man Britain Covey (knee), linebacker Jihaad Campbell and cornerback Jonathan Jones are all out, and running back Dameon Pierce went on injured reserve, leaving Jaydon Blue and practice-squad call-up Elijah Mitchell as the backups. The good news is up front and on the edge: Nolan Smith cleared the concussion protocol, so every edge rusher is available, and guard Landon Dickerson and center Drew Kendall are back after the line held together last week. The team lists no one as questionable.

  3. Opponent · Commanders

    Washington is rested after the bye, and missing McLaurin

    The Commanders (1-4-1) arrive rested after a Week 7 bye, but not whole. Terry McLaurin is on injured reserve with a torn patellar tendon in his left knee, per CBS Sports, and leading rusher Jacory Croskey-Merritt is also on IR. PhillyVoice adds that left tackle Laremy Tunsil is out with a torn triceps. Austin Ekeler is expected to take a bigger share of the carries. Defensive tackle Javon Kinlaw is out, and Daron Payne (foot) and Jer’Zhan Newton (pectoral, a possible season debut) are questionable. Jayden Daniels, who was a full participant with a left elbow listing, went 29-of-40 for 300 yards and two touchdowns in a 23-17 loss at San Francisco while facing a 39.3 percent pressure rate, per the Inquirer. He has not won a start in 390 days. Dan Quinn is on CBS Sports’ hot-seat list and called it “two angry teams willing to fight to the last snap.” AP notes the loser sits alone in last place in the NFC East. Philadelphia won the Week 1 meeting 24-22.

  4. The line · Odds

    Washington is a 4- to 6-point favorite, depending on the book

    Books agree on the favorite, not the number. BetMGM opened Washington at minus-4 on Oct. 26, and Commanders Wire and the Arizona Republic were still printing that line on Oct. 28 and 29. PhillyVoice’s Oct. 29 table has the Commanders at minus-5.5 at FanDuel, BetRivers and BetMGM and minus-6 at DraftKings and Caesars, with totals of 40 to 40.5 and Eagles moneylines from +190 to +220. Sports Illustrated shows DraftKings at minus-5.5 with the Eagles at +180, so those two outlets disagree on DraftKings’ number. AP lists Washington by 6. The Eagles are 1-6 against the spread this season and the Commanders 3-3, per AP. Models lean the same way: Dimers projects Washington 23-17 and Sports Betting Dime 23.3-20.9, and the Arizona Republic picks the Commanders 27-17. NBC considered flexing the game out of prime time, per PhillyVoice, and did not. Expect the numbers to move after Sunday’s inactives are posted about 90 minutes before kickoff.

  5. Last game · Week 7

    Led 21-20, then lost 38-21 for a fifth straight defeat

    Dallas beat the Eagles 38-21 on Monday Night Football, Philadelphia’s fifth straight loss and its first five-game skid since 2016, per NBC Sports Philadelphia. The Eagles led 21-20 after Makai Lemon’s touchdown, then were outscored 18-0. The defense allowed 416 yards, 204 on the ground, including a career-high 155 for Javonte Williams, and finished without a sack, quarterback hit or tackle for loss. Vic Fangio said, “I didn’t do a good enough job coaching.” Nick Sirianni said he saw no effort problem, though nj.com noted clips of Jalen Carter and Jordan Davis circulating online. The Athletic’s simulator gives the Eagles a 4 percent chance at the playoffs, per the Inquirer. The trade deadline is Tuesday, Nov. 10 at 4 p.m., two days after the Giants visit, and the Inquirer’s tracker says it is looking like the Eagles will be sellers. One wording conflict: the AP version carried by the Boston Globe calls this the longest skid since 2016 “when they dropped four in a row,” while NBC Sports Philadelphia and ESPN count five straight.
